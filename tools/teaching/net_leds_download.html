🟢🔴 Net LEDs

Two little lights next to the clock, like the activity LEDs on an old modem: green blinks when data comes in (download), red when it goes out (upload). Click them for a live graph of your traffic.

💻 Desktop app Windows 10 / 11 macOS 11 or later Free · MIT License

1About

What it does

  • A green light and a red light sit in the Mac menu bar or the Windows system tray, next to the clock.
  • Green blinks when data comes in (download), red when data goes out (upload). A burst of packets makes them flicker, a steady stream keeps them lit, and they glow brighter with more traffic.
  • Click the lights for a live graph of the last 60 seconds: download in green above the line, upload in red below, with the current speeds. Each side rescales itself, so slow traffic still shows.
  • Drag the graph away and it stays open as a small window while you work. Close it with the × (Windows), by clicking the lights again, or with Esc.
  • Right-click the lights (or Control-click on a Mac) for the current speeds, Open at Login (Mac) or Start with Windows, and Quit. On a Mac you can also show the speeds as numbers next to the lights.
  • It counts all your network adapters together (Wi-Fi, Ethernet…), leaving out the computer talking to itself.

Privacy

Net LEDs collects no personal data and never connects to the internet itself. It only reads the totals of bytes received and sent that your system already keeps for each network adapter. It doesn't look at what the data is or where it goes, and it records nothing: the graph only lives in memory and is gone when you quit.

Installing

  • Windows: double-click NetLEDs.exe. No installation is needed. The app isn't signed with a paid certificate, so the first time Windows may show “Windows protected your PC”. Click More info, then Run anyway. If you don't see the lights, click the ^ arrow next to the clock and drag them out to the taskbar.
  • macOS: open NetLEDs.dmg and drag Net LEDs into Applications. The app isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer ID, so the first time macOS may block it. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. If the menu bar is full, the lights may be hidden: check System Settings → Menu Bar.
