💾 Disk LED

The hard-disk activity light of an old PC, next to your clock. It blinks whenever your drives read or write, SSDs included, so you can tell at a glance when the computer is busy with the disk, or stuck on it. Click it for a live graph.

1About

What it does

  • A lamp sits in the Mac menu bar or the Windows system tray, next to the clock, like the HDD light on the front of an old computer.
  • It blinks with every burst of reading or writing on your drives, and glows brighter with more data. Light work makes it flicker; copying a big file keeps it lit.
  • Choose one lamp (amber, red, green or blue) or two lamps: green for reading and red for writing.
  • Busy shows the share of time the drives spent working. Near 100 % for a while means the drives are what's holding the computer back. On Windows it also shows the queue: operations waiting their turn.
  • Click the lamp for a live graph of the last 60 seconds: reading above the line, writing below, with the current speeds and the busy share. Drag the graph away and it stays open as a small window.
  • Drive sounds (off at first): soft synthesized clicks with the activity, like the heads of a hard disk seeking.
  • Right-click the lamp (or Control-click on a Mac) for the speeds, the lamp style, drive sounds, Open at Login (Mac) or Start with Windows, and Quit.

How it works with an SSD

The old LED lit whenever the drive was actually transferring data. Your system still counts every read and write on each drive, so Disk LED reads those counters 30 times a second. SSD operations take microseconds, far too short to see, so each burst keeps the lamp lit for a moment, which brings back the familiar flicker. Like the original, it doesn't blink for files the system already has in memory, and saved files may show a moment later, when the system writes them out.

Privacy

Disk LED collects no personal data and never connects to the internet. It only reads the activity totals your system already keeps for each drive (the ones Activity Monitor and Task Manager show). It doesn't look at which files are used, and the graph only lives in memory.

Installing

  • Windows: double-click DiskLED.exe. No installation and no administrator rights are needed. The app isn't signed with a paid certificate, so the first time Windows may show “Windows protected your PC”. Click More info, then Run anyway. If you don't see the lamp, click the ^ arrow next to the clock and drag it out to the taskbar.
  • macOS: open DiskLED.dmg and drag Disk LED into Applications. The app isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer ID, so the first time macOS may block it. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. If the menu bar is full, the lamp may be hidden: check System Settings → Menu Bar, or your menu bar manager.
