Another group has plates of the same size made of a different metal. With the same paper, their C will be…
Why do you think so? Write the reasons you both agree on.
3

Measure the plates, the paper and the leads

a · The plates

  1. Measure the length L and the width W of your plate with a ruler, in centimeters. If your two plates have different sizes, measure the smaller one: only the part where they face each other counts.
Type L and W to get the plate area A = L·W.

b · The thickness of one sheet of paper

  1. One sheet is too thin to measure. Take a stack of the same paper (the one your teacher gave you), at least 50 sheets, squeeze it gently and measure its thickness with the caliper or ruler.
  2. Count the sheets in the stack. The page divides to get the thickness t of one sheet.

c · The multimeter and the leads

  1. Turn the multimeter to capacitance (the symbol ⊣⊢ or F). Choose the unit it shows.
  2. Clip one lead to each plate and hold the plates far apart (more than 30 cm, not touching anything metal). The meter still shows a small value: that is the capacitance of the leads themselves, C₀. Write it. It will be subtracted from every reading.
  3. If your meter has a REL (relative) button and you pressed it here to zero the reading, write 0.
  4. Don't move the leads around during the lab: C₀ changes when they do.
1 nF = 1000 pF and 1 µF = 1000 nF. Your capacitor will be a few hundred to a few thousand pF (about 0.1 to 5 nF): if the meter has ranges, use the smallest one that shows the value.

Change the separation d

  1. Put the bottom plate on the table, then 1 sheet of paper, then the top plate exactly on top (full overlap). The paper must stick out on every side, so the plates never touch.
  2. Put the book on top and press it the same way every time: the air between the sheets changes the reading.
  3. Wait until the reading is steady and type it. Then use 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 sheets, the same way. Separation d = number of sheets × t.
  4. Keep 5 sheets unused: you'll need it in step 7.

Change the overlap area A

  1. Use the same number of sheets the whole time (2 or 3 is a good choice) and type it below.
  2. Slide the top plate along its length, keeping its sides lined up with the bottom plate, so that only part of it faces the bottom plate. Measure the overlap x: how many centimeters of the top plate's length are over the bottom plate.
  3. Book on top, read C. Start at full overlap (x = L) and go down to about a quarter: 5 or 6 positions. The overlap area is A = W·x.
  4. The paper must stay under the whole overlap, and the plates must never touch.

Change the material between the plates

  1. Plates exactly on top of each other (full overlap). Put one layer of a material between them: glass, plastic sheet, fabric, cardboard… It must cover the whole plate.
  2. Measure the thickness t of that layer with the caliper. For a thin sheet, measure a folded stack of several layers and divide.
  3. Book on top (for glass, press gently with your hand instead), read C. Do at least 3 materials, including 1 sheet of paper.

📋 Lab guide: Parallel-Plate Capacitor

Read this together before you start. It tells you what you'll do in this lab; the detailed instructions are inside each step of the report. You can open it again at any moment with the 📋 Lab guide button at the top.

Goal

Two metal plates separated by an insulator make a capacitor: it stores charge, and its capacitance C says how much charge it stores for each volt. Find out how C depends on the separation d between the plates, on the area A where they face each other, and on the material between them (the dielectric). Each group has plates of a different size or metal, so together the class can check that the law works for every capacitor.

What you'll do

  1. Predict (step 2): what happens to C with more paper, more area, glass, or another metal.
  2. Measure (step 3): the plates, the thickness of one sheet of paper, and the capacitance of the leads alone.
  3. Change d (step 4): 1, 2, 3 … 8 sheets of paper. Graph C against d, then against 1/d.
  4. Change A (step 5): slide one plate so they overlap less.
  5. Change the material (step 6): glass, plastic, fabric… and find the dielectric constant κ of each one.
  6. Test your model (step 7): calculate C for a new capacitor, then build it and measure.
  7. Conclude and send (steps 8 and 9). Then your class puts all the groups together.

Materials

Each group
  • Two metal plates (note their label, if they have one)
  • A multimeter that measures capacitance, and two leads with clips
  • Sheets of paper (all groups use the same paper) and a stack of it to measure
  • A sheet of glass, of plastic, a piece of fabric, cardboard…
  • A ruler, a caliper, a heavy book, and this page open on one computer
Check before you start
  • The plates are flat, clean and not bent: bent plates leave air gaps
  • The paper and the other materials are bigger than the plates, so the plates can never touch
  • Circuit-board plates: the copper sides face each other
Safety: handle the glass by its faces and don't press hard on it; tell your teacher at once if it breaks. Plate edges can be sharp. The meter uses a tiny voltage, so the plates are safe to touch, but don't touch them while it reads: your hand changes the reading.
