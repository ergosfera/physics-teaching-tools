3

The model

When the two waters mix, energy flows from the hot water to the cold water until both reach the same temperature, the equilibrium temperature Teq. The energy needed to change the temperature of a mass m by ΔT is Q = m·c·ΔT. If no energy escapes:

heat lost by the hot water = heat gained by the cold water
mh · c · (Th − Teq) = mc · c · (Teq − Tc)

c = 4186 J/(kg·°C) is the specific heat of water. Solve this equation for Teq in your notebook. You will use it to predict the result of every trial.

Practice: 150 g of water at 70 °C is mixed with 50 g of water at 30 °C. Use your equation: what is Teq?

Trials: predict, mix, measure

Do at least 2 trials, each with a different proportion of hot and cold water. The microwave and the balance are shared by the class, so plan ahead and be ready when it's your turn.

  1. Cold beaker: put the empty beaker on the balance, press tare (zero), and add tap water. Write the water mass, then measure its temperature Tc.
  2. Hot beaker: tare the second beaker, add water and weigh it. Heat it in the microwave (aim for 50–70 °C; never let it boil). Handle it with care.
  3. Stir the hot water and measure Th right before pouring.
  4. Use the model to calculate your prediction and lock it.
  5. Pour all the hot water into the cold beaker, stir, and write the highest steady temperature the thermometer reaches.

Each point is one trial. Across: the fraction of the water that was hot. Up: how far the mixture moved from Tc toward Th (0 = stayed at Tc, 1 = reached Th). If the model were perfect, every point would sit on the dashed diagonal.
