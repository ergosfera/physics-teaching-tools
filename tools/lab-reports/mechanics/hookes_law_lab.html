📋 Lab guide: Hooke's Law

Read this together before you start. You can open it again at any moment with the 📋 Lab guide button at the top.

Goal

Find out how the stretch of a spring depends on the force that pulls it, and measure the spring constant k of your spring.

Materials

Each pair
  • A spring (note its label, if it has one)
  • A universal support: base, rod and clamp or hook
  • A ruler with millimeter divisions
  • A set of masses and a mass hanger
  • This page, open on one computer
Check before you start
  • The mass written on each mass (and on the hanger)
  • The maximum load your teacher allows for your spring
  • That the support doesn't wobble

Setup

  1. Screw the rod into the base and fix the clamp near the top of the rod. Check that the support is stable.
  2. Hang the spring from the clamp or hook so it hangs freely, without touching the rod.
  3. Fix the ruler vertically right next to the spring (clamp or tape it to the rod). It must not move during the whole experiment.
  4. Choose two reference points on the spring, for example the top of the top loop and the bottom of the bottom hook. The length L is always measured between these same two points.
  5. Decide who hangs the masses, who reads the ruler, and who types.

Procedure

  1. With nothing hanging, measure the natural length L₀ between your reference points (step 3 on the page).
  2. Hang the hanger with the first mass. Wait until the spring is completely still, read L at eye level, and type the total mass and L (step 4).
  3. Add masses one at a time, for example in 50 g steps, recording each. Aim for 6 to 8 measurements spread over the allowed range. Never exceed the maximum load.
  4. Watch the graph build as you measure, and answer the discussion question when it appears.
  5. Keep one combination of masses unused: you'll need it in step 6 to test your model.
  6. When you finish, remove all the masses and check that the spring goes back to L₀. If it doesn't, it was overstretched: tell your teacher.
Safety: keep hands and feet away from under the hanging masses, add and remove masses gently, and make sure the support can't tip over.
