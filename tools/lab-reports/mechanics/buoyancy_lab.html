3

Measure the cylinder and your liquid

  1. Decide who moves the cylinder, who reads the balance, and who types.
  2. Measure the diameter d of the cylinder with the caliper (or ruler) in two or three places and write the average.
  3. Check the depth marks on the cylinder: the 0 mark must be at the bottom face. If it has no marks, draw one every centimeter with a ruler and a permanent marker.
Type the diameter to get the cross-section area A = π·d²/4.

Density of your liquid

  1. Put an empty measuring cylinder on the balance and press TARE (zero).
  2. Pour in about 50 mL of your liquid. Read the volume V at the bottom of the meniscus and the mass m on the balance.
4

Measure: change the volume under the liquid

  1. Fill a beaker about two thirds with your liquid and put it on the balance. Press TARE: from now on the balance shows only the change.
  2. Fix the cylinder in the clamp, vertical, right above the center of the beaker. It must never touch the bottom or the walls.
  3. Lower the clamp until the liquid reaches the first mark. Wait until the reading is steady, then type the depth h and the balance reading.
  4. Go deeper one mark at a time. Aim for 6 to 8 measurements, and keep one depth between two marks unused: you'll need it in step 6. Read h where the liquid surface meets the marks: the liquid rises when the cylinder goes in, and reading at the surface takes that into account.

Why is the balance reading a force? The liquid pushes the cylinder up with the buoyant force Fb, so the cylinder pushes the liquid down just as hard (Newton's third law), and the balance feels it. A reading of R grams means Fb = (R / 1000)·g.

📋 Lab guide: Buoyant Force

Read this together before you start. It tells you what you'll do in this lab; the detailed instructions are inside each step of the report. You can open it again at any moment with the 📋 Lab guide button at the top.

Goal

A liquid pushes up on anything you put in it: the buoyant force. Find out how it depends on the volume of the object under the liquid. Each group measures a different liquid, so together the class can also find out how it depends on the density of the liquid.

What you'll do

  1. Predict (step 2): what the balance does when the cylinder goes in, deeper, or into a denser liquid.
  2. Measure (step 3): the diameter of the cylinder and the density of your liquid.
  3. Change the volume (step 4): lower the cylinder into your liquid mark by mark and record the balance. The graph of Fb against V builds as you go.
  4. Analyze (step 5): find the slope, divide it by the density, and write the law.
  5. Test your model (step 6): predict a balance reading, then measure it.
  6. Conclude and send (steps 7 and 8). Then your class compares all the liquids together.

Materials

Each pair
  • A solid cylinder with depth marks (note its label, if it has one)
  • A support with a clamp to hold the cylinder
  • A balance (0.1 g or better) and a caliper or ruler
  • A beaker, a measuring cylinder, and your group's liquid
  • Paper towels, and this page open on one computer
Check before you start
  • Which liquid your teacher assigned to your group
  • The cylinder hangs vertically and can go down without hitting the bottom or the walls
  • You know how to TARE (zero) the balance
Safety: wipe up spills at once (oil and salt water make the floor slippery), keep liquids away from the computer, keep alcohol away from flames, and don't taste anything.
