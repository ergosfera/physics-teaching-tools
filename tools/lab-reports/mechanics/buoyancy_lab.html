Measure the cylinder and the liquids

  1. Decide who moves the cylinder, who reads the balance, and who types.
  2. Measure the diameter d of the cylinder with the caliper (or ruler) in two or three places and write the average.
  3. Check the depth marks on the cylinder: the 0 mark must be at the bottom face. If it has no marks, draw one every centimeter with a ruler and a permanent marker.
Type the diameter to get the cross-section area A = π·d²/4.

Density of each liquid

  1. You need water and at least two other liquids (for example salt water, vegetable oil, alcohol). Ask your teacher which ones you have.
  2. Put an empty measuring cylinder on the balance and press TARE (zero).
  3. Pour in about 50 mL of the liquid. Read the volume V at the bottom of the meniscus and the mass m on the balance.
  4. Empty, rinse and dry the measuring cylinder, and repeat for every liquid.

4

Measure in water: change the volume

  1. Fill a beaker about two thirds with water and put it on the balance. Press TARE: from now on the balance shows only the change.
  2. Fix the cylinder in the clamp, vertical, right above the center of the beaker. It must never touch the bottom or the walls.
  3. Lower the clamp until the water reaches the first mark. Wait until the reading is steady, then type the depth h and the balance reading.
  4. Go deeper one mark at a time. Aim for 6 to 8 measurements. Read h where the water surface meets the marks: the water rises when the cylinder goes in, and reading at the surface takes that into account.
#Depth h (cm)Balance reading (g)Volume under water V = A·h (cm³)Buoyant force Fb (N)
Why is the balance reading a force? The liquid pushes the cylinder up with the buoyant force Fb, so the cylinder pushes the liquid down just as hard (Newton's third law), and the balance feels it. A reading of R grams means Fb = (R / 1000)·g.

📋 Lab guide: Buoyant Force

Read this together before you start. It tells you what you'll do in this lab; the detailed instructions are inside each step of the report. You can open it again at any moment with the 📋 Lab guide button at the top.

Goal

A liquid pushes up on anything you put in it: the buoyant force. Find out how it depends on the volume of the object under the liquid and on the density of the liquid, and write the law yourselves.

What you'll do

  1. Predict (step 2): what the balance does when the cylinder goes in, deeper, or into a denser liquid.
  2. Measure (step 3): the diameter of the cylinder and the density of each liquid.
  3. Change the volume (step 4): lower the cylinder into water mark by mark and record the balance. The graph of Fb against V builds as you go.
  4. Change the density (step 5): repeat in at least two other liquids and compare the lines.
  5. Analyze (step 6): plot the slope of each line against the density of its liquid, and find the law.
  6. Test your model (step 7): predict a balance reading, then measure it.
  7. Conclude and send (steps 8 and 9).

Materials

Each pair
  • A solid cylinder with depth marks (note its label, if it has one)
  • A support with a clamp to hold the cylinder
  • A balance (0.1 g or better) and a caliper or ruler
  • Beakers, a measuring cylinder, and water plus at least two other liquids
  • Paper towels, and this page open on one computer
Check before you start
  • The cylinder hangs vertically and can go down without hitting the bottom or the walls
  • The balance is on a flat table and nobody leans on it
  • You know how to TARE (zero) the balance
Safety: wipe up spills at once (oil and salt water make the floor slippery), keep liquids away from the computer, keep alcohol away from flames, and don't taste anything.
