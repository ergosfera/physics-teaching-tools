🪐 Gravitation Lab

① Your gravitational system

Each pair studies one system: a central body and the things that orbit it. Find the data in a reliable source and type it into the table. At the end, the whole class puts its systems together.

Write large numbers like 1.898e27 (meaning 1.898 × 10²⁷). A decimal comma works too.
📚 Where to find the data
  • Planets around the Sun: NASA Planetary Fact Sheet. Use "Distance from Sun" (10⁶ km) and "Mean Orbital Velocity" (km/s).
  • Moons of a planet: JPL Planetary Satellite Mean Elements. Use the semi-major axis a (km) and the period (days).
  • Mass of the central body: the same NASA fact sheets, or JPL Planetary Physical Parameters.
  • Earth's artificial satellites: UCS Satellite Database. It lists altitude above the ground and period in minutes. R must be measured from Earth's centre, so add Earth's radius (6371 km).
  • Exoplanets: NASA Exoplanet Archive. Use the semi-major axis (AU), the orbital period (days) and the star's mass.

Avoid bodies on very stretched orbits, such as comets: the method assumes nearly circular motion.

R is the mean orbital distance (semi-major axis). If your source gives the period T, the tool works out v = 2πR/T. The optional eccentricity e gives Δr, how much the farthest distance exceeds the nearest: (rmax − rmin)/rmin. A large Δr means the orbit is far from a circle. Untick bodies you decide to leave out.

② Rotation curve

Astronomers study galaxies with a rotation curve: the orbital speed v of each object plotted against its distance R from the centre. Do the same for your system.

  • How does v change as R grows?
  • Is the curve a straight line? Does it ever reach zero?
  • If R doubles, what happens to v? Find two bodies where one is about twice as far as the other.
  • Switch to log–log axes. What happens to the shape?
Hover over or tap a point to read its values.

③ Find the pattern

Try a power law v = k · Rⁿ. Move the exponent n until the curve goes through all the points. k is chosen automatically for each n.

Next: what stays constant?

If v = k·Rⁿ, then v·R⁻ⁿ = k is the same number for every body in your system. With the n you found, square both sides and you get a simpler combination of v and R, with no roots in it. Which one?

The table on the right shows v²R for each body. Its average is your system's constant. You'll report it in step ④.

Discuss

  • Your value of Γ is not exactly the accepted G. List the sources of error: rounded data, non-circular orbits, how the fits were done…
  • On Earth's system, the Moon's v²R is about 1% higher than the artificial satellites'. Why might a heavy moon behave differently from a light satellite?
  • Which system has the most reliable points? Which would you leave out, and why?
  • The law only needs M, m and R. Use it backwards: how could you find the mass of a planet from one of its moons?
  • Newton did not have a spreadsheet. What did he have instead?

⚙ Class connection

Without a connection, pairs share results as copy-and-paste codes. To collect results live, the teacher connects the lab to a Google Sheet once (steps below). Students then open the student link and their results appear on everyone's class board.

Teacher setup: Google Sheet + Apps Script (about 10 minutes, once)
  1. Create a new Google Sheet (type sheets.new in the address bar). Name it, e.g. Gravitation Lab results.
  2. In the Sheet: Extensions → Apps Script. Delete the sample code, paste the script below, and click 💾 Save.
  3. Deploy → New deployment. Click ⚙ next to "Select type" and choose Web app. Set Execute as: Me, Who has access: Anyone. Click Deploy and authorise with your Google account. Google will warn that the app is unverified, because it's your own script: Advanced → Go to … (unsafe).
  4. Copy the Web app URL, paste it above, choose a session code, and click Save and connect.
  5. Click Copy student link and share it (Classroom, QR code, board). Students who open it are already connected.

Results land in a Results tab: time, session, team, system, mass, v²R. A pair that sends again replaces its earlier row. To clean up, delete rows in the Sheet. If you edit the script later, use Deploy → Manage deployments → ✏ → New version so the URL stays the same. Only team names and numbers are stored, no student accounts.
