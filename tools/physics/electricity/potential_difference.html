⚡ Potential Difference

Lifting a ball from level A to level B stores energy in the ball–Earth system. How much depends on the mass. But the energy per kilogram depends only on the two levels.

Same two levels, different masses
What to notice

The dashed lines are levels: every point on one line has the same value of g·h. Moving sideways along a line costs no energy.

Change the mass: ΔU changes, but ΔU / m stays the same. The number g·Δh describes the two levels, not the ball. We could call it the gravitational potential difference, measured in J/kg.

Going up, you do positive work and the stored energy grows. Going down, gravity does the work and the stored energy shrinks.

Two charged plates make a uniform field, just like gravity near the ground. Moving a charge from A to B changes the stored energy. The energy per coulomb is the potential difference, or voltage.

The analogy
mass m↔charge q
field g (N/kg)↔field E (N/C)
U = m·g·h↔U = q·E·y
g·h (J/kg)↔V = E·y (J/C = volt)
ΔU = m·(gΔh)↔ΔU = q·ΔV
What to notice

The dashed lines are equipotentials: the electric version of levels. Every point on one line has the same voltage, so moving a charge along it takes no work.

Make q negative. The energy change flips sign, but ΔV does not: ΔV belongs to the two places, not to the charge. Gravity has only one kind of mass, so it never shows this.

1 volt means 1 joule for every coulomb that crosses between the two places.

Let go, and the stored energy becomes kinetic energy. A ball falls through a height; a charged particle "falls" through a voltage.

Particle
What to notice

For the ball, ½mv² = m·g·h, so the mass cancels: every ball dropped from the same height lands with the same speed.

For the particle, ½mv² = q·ΔV. Here q/m matters: an electron and a proton gain exactly the same energy through the same voltage, but the much lighter electron ends up far faster.

The electron is negative, so it "falls" from the − plate toward the + plate: from low potential to high potential. The plates are flipped for it so it still falls downward on screen.

Electronvolt: the energy one elementary charge gains across 1 V. 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

About the animation

Both motions are shown in the same 1.6 s so they can be compared. The real particle crosses the gap in nanoseconds. Gravity on the particle is ignored: the electric force is about 10¹² times larger.

Around a single charge the levels are no longer flat. A positive charge makes a hill of potential; a negative charge makes a valley, like the gravity well of a planet. Drag A and B.

Path from A to B
Potential along a line through Q
What to notice

The circles are equipotentials, like the contour lines on a hiking map. Close circles mean a steep hill: a strong field.

The field E always points "downhill", from high V to low V, and crosses the equipotentials at right angles.

The work along the straight path and along the bent path is the same. Only the start and end levels matter, just as the energy to climb a mountain depends only on the change in altitude, not on the trail.

Far away, V → 0. That is our reference level, like choosing the ground as h = 0.
