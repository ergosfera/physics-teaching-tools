What is going on?

On flat ground, a ball launched at 45° travels a range v²/g: ten times faster means a hundred times farther.

When the range becomes hundreds of kilometers, the Earth's curvature matters: the ground drops away below the ball while it falls, so it lands farther than the flat-ground formula predicts.

At about 7.9 km/s (fired horizontally), the ground curves away exactly as fast as the ball falls. The ball keeps falling forever and never reaches the ground: it is in orbit. Faster still, about 11.2 km/s, and it escapes the Earth.

Why can't a 45° shot from the ground orbit? A closed orbit always comes back to the point where it started, and it dips below that height on the way. To orbit, the ball must be launched horizontally from above the ground — Newton's mountain.

What the simulation leaves out

No air resistance and no Earth rotation. The cannon, the ball and the mountain are not drawn to scale: they keep the same size on screen while the view zooms. The continents are a rough sketch, and the cannon sits in northern Québec so the Americas face you. Gravity is Newton's inverse-square law, so g weakens as the ball climbs.
