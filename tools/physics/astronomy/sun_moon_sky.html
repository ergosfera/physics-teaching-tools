Physics notes

Altitude is the angle above the horizon; azimuth is measured from North through East (N 0°, E 90°, S 180°, W 270°).

Rise / set = the upper edge touches the horizon, including ≈34′ of atmospheric refraction (h₀ = −0.83° for the Sun). Twilight ends when the Sun is 6°, 12° and 18° below the horizon.

The Moon moves ≈13° east per day along its orbit, so it rises on average ≈50 min later each day.

The lit side of the Moon always faces the Sun, but its tilt in your sky depends on latitude: near the equator a crescent lies like a boat; in the southern hemisphere the Moon looks upside-down compared with the north.

Clouds decide whether you can see anything at all. Low clouds (below ≈2 km) block the sky; thin high clouds (cirrus) often still let the Moon and bright planets through. A bright Moon washes out faint stars, so the best stargazing nights are clear and moonless.

Low-precision Meeus algorithms: positions within a few arcminutes, times within ≈1–2 min.
